04 Featured case study

Inventory & Order Tracking: a business analysis.

I defined requirements for a small supplier's inventory and ordering system, built the database, and analyzed it to answer operations questions: what to reorder, what earns margin, where shipping is slow.

Practice sample. The company, products and numbers are hypothetical, created for this exercise. They are not employer data and not work experience. The analysis, SQL and documents are real.

The problem

A small wholesale supplier tracks stock and orders in spreadsheets. Staff learn about low stock only when an order can't be filled, some regions ship slowly, and managers can't see which products or customers drive profit.

StakeholderNeeds
Operations managerFewer stock-outs, faster shipping
Warehouse staffClear pick/ship queue, accurate counts
PurchasingReorder list with supplier lead times
FinanceRevenue and margin by category and customer

As-is vs to-be

Order by email→Check spreadsheet→Manual pick list→Ship→Late status update
Order entered once→Stock check + shortfall flag→Generated pick list→Status with timestamps→Daily reorder list

Functional requirements

  • FR1 Record orders with customer, date, status and line items.
  • FR2 Reduce on-hand quantity when an order is picked; never allow negative stock.
  • FR3 List products at or below reorder point with supplier and lead time.
  • FR4 Report revenue and margin by category and customer, excluding cancelled orders.
  • FR5 Report average days from order to shipment by region.

Non-functional

  • NFR1 Reports refresh daily and run in under 5 seconds for 10,000 orders.
  • NFR2 Only authorized staff can change stock counts; changes are logged.

User stories & acceptance criteria

StoryAcceptance criteria
As purchasing, I want a daily reorder list so I can order before stock runs out.Products at or below reorder point appear with supplier and lead time, largest shortfall first.
As a picker, I want stock to drop when I mark an order Picked so counts stay accurate.On Picked, on-hand falls by order quantity; if quantity exceeds on-hand the pick is blocked and the shortfall shown.
As finance, I want margin by category so I can see what drives profit.Cancelled orders excluded. Margin % = (price − cost) / price, rounded to 0.1.
As the operations manager, I want shipping time by region so I can find slow lanes.Only shipped or delivered orders count; show order count and average days.

What to reorder

Red marker = reorder point. Bar = current stock.

Coffee Beans are at 35 vs a reorder point of 40, from the supplier with the longest lead time (7 days). Order them first.

Gross margin by category (%)

Beverage is the largest category but has the lowest margin (50.4%). Merchandise has the highest (65.1%). Review beverage pricing or cost.

Average days to ship, by region

South took 6.0 days vs 1.0 in the North, but the South has only one shipped order here, so I'd gather more data before acting.
